<objective>
Open round 3 of the fix process of Phase 4. The independent review of round 2 returned three BLOCKING findings, and the repository owner chose Fix for all three; the outcome is `fix pending` and the phase cannot close. This plan (1) gives the fix round script a profile for round 3 and a replay of round 2, so that nothing already proven is lost when the text changes; (2) asks the owner, once, how each of the three problems is to be fixed; and (3) edits the specification for those three problems only, in the way the owner chose, and records what changed and why in a short register.

Purpose: roadmap Phase 4 is a hard gate and its third success criterion is that blocking findings are resolved (fixed) or explicitly dispositioned by the owner before the diff is treated as final (REVIEW-02). A fix edits the approved text, which voids the approval of it; the text must be approved again (plan 04-11) and reviewed again (plan 04-12), and the new findings dispositioned (plan 04-13). This round does that with as little machinery and as few owner questions as the non-negotiable rules allow.

Output: the extended fix round script, the edited specification, the register, a summary.

## Plain language for the owner (applies to every owner-facing question of plans 04-10 to 04-13)

(1) Before it asks, the orchestrator explains in a few sentences of everyday words, with one concrete example, what is being asked and what each answer leads to; section numbers, field names, issue codes, hashes and finding ids appear only where the owner needs them to find something. The explanation says "the reviewer says" and never whether the reviewer is right (the Requestor assesses no finding); it is not committed. (2) A question is at most two sentences. (3) Option labels are at most four words, each with one short description; no Gain and Cost clauses. (4) No option is marked as recommended. (5) If the owner says a question is unclear, the orchestrator does not send it again: it explains in the chat and asks what would help. (6) Where a check compares a recorded question or label with a printed one, the printed one is the plain text, so the owner is shown exactly what is recorded. (7) A name and a date are typed by the owner; nothing is suggested or prefilled, and only if the owner explicitly asks for candidates does the orchestrator offer them, the owner picks one, and the record says so.
